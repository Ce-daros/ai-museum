Sentence splitting is naive (based on punctuation), so very unusual punctuation may behave oddly. All processing is case-insensitive; output sentences are shown as in the original text.

Luhn Summarizer (1958) Frequency + Clusters

Significant words are selected based on term frequency (with stopwords removed). Sentences are scored via significance factors computed from clusters of significant words where the gap between successive significant words is below a maximum distance. For each cluster, score = (sigCount² / clusterLength); the sentence score is the maximum cluster score.

Edmundson Summarizer (1969) Cue + Key + Title + Position

Each sentence is scored using four indicators: Cue (bonus/stigma cue words), Key (key words derived from frequency), Title (overlap with title), and Position (location in document). Indicators are normalized and combined via a linear weighting scheme.

Key words are approximated as the top non-stopword terms by frequency within the document. Cue words include a small list of bonus and stigma words inspired by Edmundson's formulation.

All computation happens locally in your browser. Implementations aim to follow the core structure of the original algorithms: Luhn's significance factor with clustering of significant words, and Edmundson's combination of Cue/Key/Title/Position indicators for sentence extraction.
